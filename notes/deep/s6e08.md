## Key points
- Revision episode: interview questions on **real-time transports**.
- Know what goes wrong with `setInterval` polling, why a 7th SSE tab hangs, which transport serves a million viewers, and how a WebRTC offer reaches the other peer.

## Interview answer
> I choose transports by direction and frequency, and I design for the failure modes of each: polling must not overlap and should pause when hidden, SSE needs HTTP/2 or a shared stream when many tabs are open, WebSockets need reconnect logic, and WebRTC needs my own signaling plus STUN and TURN servers.

## Quick quiz
<details><summary>A setInterval poll misbehaves when the server is slow. Why, and the fix?</summary>

Requests overlap and responses can arrive out of order. Schedule the next poll with setTimeout after the response, back off on errors, and pause when the tab is hidden.
</details>
<details><summary>The 7th tab with an SSE stream never connects. Why?</summary>

HTTP/1.1 allows about 6 connections per origin and each stream holds one. Use HTTP/2 (multiplexing) or share one stream across tabs (e.g. via BroadcastChannel or a SharedWorker).
</details>
<details><summary>Live scores for a million viewers: which transport?</summary>

SSE: one-way server push over plain HTTP, with automatic reconnect, and it scales with standard HTTP infrastructure and fan-out.
</details>
<details><summary>How does a WebRTC offer reach the other browser?</summary>

Through your own signaling channel (often a WebSocket server); WebRTC has no built-in signaling. STUN/TURN then help establish the actual connection.
</details>
