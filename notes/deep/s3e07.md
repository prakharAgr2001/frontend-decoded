## Key points
- **Lab data** (Lighthouse): one run, simulated mid-range phone, throttled network, clean browser. **Repeatable**, good for debugging.
- **Field data** (CrUX, RUM): **real users** on real devices, over 28 days, at p75. This is what users and Google actually judge you on.
- They often disagree, and **field data wins**.

## Interview answer
> Lab tools like Lighthouse run a controlled test on a simulated device, which makes them repeatable and great for diagnosing issues, but they only cover the initial load, one device profile and no real interactions, so there's no INP. Field data comes from real users, through the Chrome UX Report or my own real user monitoring with the web-vitals library, aggregated at the 75th percentile. I use field data to decide what's wrong and lab tools to debug and verify fixes.

## The analogy
A car's fuel economy on the official test track versus what you actually get in Bangalore traffic. The test is useful for comparing cars; your real mileage is what matters to your wallet.

## How it works
```compare
Lab (Lighthouse) || Field (CrUX / RUM)
One simulated device and network || Thousands of real devices and networks
Only the first page load || Whole visit, including interactions
No INP (TBT as a proxy) || Real INP
Instant results || 28-day rolling window
Debug and verify || Judge and prioritise
```


## Common traps
- **"Lighthouse 98, but Search Console says poor."** Real users have slower devices and interact with the page; lab can't see that.
- **Expecting fixes to show instantly.** CrUX is a 28-day window, so improvements fade in over weeks.
- **Low-traffic pages** may have no CrUX data; your own RUM fills the gap.

## Quick quiz
<details><summary>Why can't Lighthouse measure INP?</summary>

It loads the page without interacting with it, so there are no clicks or taps to measure. It reports TBT as a proxy.
</details>
<details><summary>What's CrUX?</summary>

The Chrome UX Report: field performance data from opted-in Chrome users, aggregated per origin/URL over 28 days.
</details>
<details><summary>When do you use lab tools if field data is what counts?</summary>

To debug, reproduce and verify fixes quickly in a controlled environment before field data catches up.
</details>
