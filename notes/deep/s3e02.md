## Key points
- Google's **Core Web Vitals** are three metrics for real user experience:
- **LCP** (loading) ≤ **2.5 s** · **INP** (responsiveness) ≤ **200 ms** · **CLS** (visual stability) ≤ **0.1**.
- They're graded at the **75th percentile of real Chrome visits** (CrUX data), separately for mobile and desktop. INP replaced FID in March 2024.

## Interview answer
> Core Web Vitals are Google's user-centric metrics. Largest Contentful Paint measures loading, with good at 2.5 seconds or less. Interaction to Next Paint measures responsiveness to clicks, taps and key presses, with good at 200 ms or less. Cumulative Layout Shift measures unexpected movement, with good at 0.1 or less. They're assessed at the 75th percentile of real user visits from the Chrome UX Report, mobile and desktop separately, and they're a factor in Google Search ranking.

## The analogy
A restaurant review with three scores: **how fast your food arrived** (LCP), **how quickly the waiter responds when you call** (INP), and **whether the table wobbles and spills your drink** (CLS).

## How it works
```compare
Metric || Good / Poor
LCP: biggest content element painted || ≤ 2.5 s / > 4 s
INP: interaction → next paint || ≤ 200 ms / > 500 ms
CLS: unexpected layout movement || ≤ 0.1 / > 0.25
```

- A page "passes" when **all three** are good at p75.
- Data comes from real Chrome users over a rolling 28-day window (CrUX), so fixes take weeks to show fully.

## Common traps
- **FID is gone.** INP replaced it in March 2024 and is much harder to pass.
- **Lab ≠ field.** Lighthouse can't measure INP (no real interactions); use field data to judge.
- **Mobile and desktop are separate.** Passing on desktop says little about mobile.

## See it yourself
**PageSpeed Insights** → enter any URL → the top section "Discover what your real users are experiencing" shows field LCP, INP and CLS at p75.
