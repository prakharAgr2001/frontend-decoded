## Key points
- Speed is money: Google's research found **53% of mobile visits are abandoned** when a page takes over 3 seconds. An often-quoted Amazon test found **+100 ms ≈ −1% sales**.
- Human time limits: **0.1 s** feels instant, **1 s** keeps you in flow, **10 s** and attention is gone.
- Performance has two jobs: **be fast** (send less, do less) and **feel fast** (show useful content early, respond instantly).

## Interview answer
> Performance directly affects business metrics like conversion and bounce rate, especially on mobile networks. I think of it as two goals: actually being fast, by sending fewer bytes and doing less work on the main thread, and feeling fast, by showing meaningful content early and giving immediate feedback on interactions. I measure with real-user data at the 75th percentile rather than averages, because averages hide the slow users who are most likely to leave.

## The analogy
A queue at a bank. Even if total service time is the same, a counter that greets you instantly and hands you a token *feels* faster than one where you stand in silence. Real speed and perceived speed both matter.

## How it works
```steps
0.1 s | feels instant: button presses, typing
1 s | still feels connected: page transitions
10 s | attention lost: users leave or switch tabs
```

- **Be fast:** smaller bundles, compressed images, fewer requests, caching, less main-thread work.
- **Feel fast:** skeletons instead of spinners, render above-the-fold first, instant button feedback, optimistic UI.

## Common traps
- **Using averages.** An average of 1.5 s can hide 25% of users waiting 5 s. Report the **75th percentile (p75)**.
- **Testing on a fast laptop and fibre.** Most users are on mid-range Android phones and mobile data.
- **Spinners for everything.** A skeleton that matches the final layout feels faster than a blank spinner.

## See it yourself
DevTools → **Network** → throttling **Slow 4G** (and **Performance** → CPU 4x slowdown) → reload your site. That's closer to what many users see.

## Quick quiz
<details><summary>Why report p75 instead of the average?</summary>

Averages hide the slow tail. p75 means 75% of visits were at least this fast, so it captures the experience of slower users too.
</details>
<details><summary>What's the difference between being fast and feeling fast?</summary>

Being fast reduces actual load and processing time. Feeling fast changes what users see and when (early content, instant feedback), so waiting is less noticeable.
</details>
<details><summary>What are the 0.1 s, 1 s and 10 s limits?</summary>

0.1 s feels instantaneous, 1 s keeps the user's flow of thought, 10 s is the limit of attention.
</details>
