## Key points
- **WebRTC** connects **browser to browser** for audio, video and data.
- Before connecting, the peers exchange an **offer/answer (SDP)** and **ICE candidates** through a **signaling** channel you build (usually a WebSocket).
- **STUN** tells a browser its public address; **TURN** relays traffic when a direct connection is impossible.

## Interview answer
> WebRTC enables peer-to-peer media and data between browsers. It has no built-in signaling, so peers exchange session descriptions, the offer and answer, and network candidates through my own server, typically over a WebSocket. ICE then tries to find a direct path using STUN servers to discover public addresses, and falls back to a TURN relay if NAT or firewalls block direct connections. Mesh calls don't scale because each participant uploads a stream to every other participant, so larger calls use an SFU that receives one upload and forwards it.

## The analogy
Two people who want to meet in a crowded city. A mutual friend passes messages between them ("I'm near the clock tower", "I'm at gate 3") until they find each other. After that, they talk directly. If they truly can't reach each other, they meet through the friend (TURN relay).

## How it works
```steps
Caller | new RTCPeerConnection({ iceServers: [STUN, TURN] })
Add media or data | addTrack(camera) or createDataChannel('chat')
Create offer | createOffer → setLocalDescription → send via your signaling server
Callee answers | setRemoteDescription → createAnswer → send back
Exchange ICE candidates | both sides, via signaling
Connected | media and data flow peer to peer (or via TURN)
```

```js
const pc = new RTCPeerConnection({ iceServers: [{ urls: 'stun:stun.l.google.com:19302' }] });
const channel = pc.createDataChannel('chat');
pc.onicecandidate = e => e.candidate && signaling.send({ candidate: e.candidate });
const offer = await pc.createOffer();
await pc.setLocalDescription(offer);
signaling.send({ offer });                        // your WebSocket delivers it to the other peer
```

## Common traps
- **"WebRTC handles signaling."** It doesn't; you build it.
- **No TURN server**: calls fail for users behind strict corporate or mobile NATs.
- **Mesh for big calls**: with 100 people, each uploads 99 streams. Use an SFU.

## See it yourself
During a Google Meet call, open `chrome://webrtc-internals` in another tab to see the peer connection, candidates and live bitrate graphs.

## Quick quiz
<details><summary>What is signaling in WebRTC?</summary>

Exchanging offers, answers and ICE candidates between peers through a channel you provide, often a WebSocket server.
</details>
<details><summary>STUN vs TURN?</summary>

STUN helps a peer discover its public IP/port; TURN relays the traffic when a direct connection can't be established.
</details>
<details><summary>Why don't big video calls use a mesh?</summary>

Each participant would upload a separate stream to every other participant; an SFU needs only one upload per participant.
</details>
