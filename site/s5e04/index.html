Backend for Frontend (BFF)

2 min read

Key points

  • A Backend for Frontend (BFF) is a small server owned by the frontend team. The app makes one call; the BFF calls several services in parallel, close to them, trims the fields, and returns screen-shaped data.
  • Usually there's one BFF per experience (mobile vs web).
  • Bonus: tokens stay on the server, and the browser just holds an httpOnly session cookie.

Interview answer

A BFF is a thin backend layer dedicated to one frontend. Instead of the client calling several microservices and stitching data together over a slow mobile network, the client calls the BFF once; the BFF fans out to services over the fast internal network, aggregates and trims the responses into exactly what the screen needs, and can also handle auth by keeping tokens server-side. It should only shape data; business rules like pricing must stay in the core services, or they'll drift between BFFs.

The analogy

A personal shopper at a mall. Instead of you walking to four shops, you give one list to the shopper, who runs to all four at once and hands you one bag with exactly what you asked for.

How it works

// BFF route (Node/Express)
app.get('/screens/home', async (req, res) => {
  const [user, orders, offers] = await Promise.all([
    users.get(req.session.userId),
    ordersApi.recent(req.session.userId, 3),
    offersApi.forUser(req.session.userId),
  ]);
  res.json({ name: user.firstName, orders: orders.map(o => ({ id: o.id, status: o.status })), offers });
});

Common traps

  • Business logic in the BFF (pricing, discounts) gets copied into each BFF and drifts. Keep BFFs to shaping only.
  • One giant BFF for every client defeats the purpose.
  • Sequential calls inside the BFF waste its advantage. Run them in parallel.

See it yourself

On a big app like Swiggy or Flipkart, DevTools → Network: home screens often load from one aggregated endpoint instead of many separate service calls.

Quick quiz

What problem does a BFF solve for mobile apps?

It replaces many slow client-to-service round trips with one call; the BFF aggregates services over the fast internal network.

What shouldn't go in a BFF?

Core business rules (pricing, permissions logic) that would get duplicated across BFFs.

How can a BFF improve security?

It keeps access tokens server-side and gives the browser only an httpOnly session cookie.

  • A small server owned by the frontend team: app makes one call; BFF fans out to services (in parallel, close to them), trims fields, returns screen-shaped data.
  • Usually one BFF per experience (mobile vs web). Bonus: keeps tokens on the server, browser gets an httpOnly cookie.
  • Trap: no business rules (pricing etc.) in the BFF — they get copied per BFF and drift. BFF = shaping only.
